About

Backend focused Computer Science student at Manchester Metropolitan University, graduating 2027. I build systems where money and state have to stay correct under failure. Lead project: NovaBank (FastAPI + PostgreSQL double entry ledger with row locks, idempotent transfers, and pytest on the money path).

Also built ChatWire for real time messaging with auth, cursor pagination, and rate limits. This site is the record of what I shipped, how it works, and what I learned.

Seeking graduate backend software engineering roles in London or Manchester from 2027.

More about me

I've always been the person who wants to know how things work.

While studying for my Extended Diploma in Engineering at college, I was originally considering going into mechanical or electrical engineering. As part of the course, I started working with Arduino boards and small hardware projects. I enjoyed writing code that made something physical happen and seeing the result straight away. That was what first got me seriously interested in software.

I liked being able to take an idea, write some code, test it, change it, and see what happened. The more I worked with it, the more I found myself interested in the programming side of the projects.

That eventually led me to study Computer Science at Manchester Metropolitan University. I wanted to learn more than just how to write code. I wanted to understand how software is designed, how different parts of a system work together, and how to build things properly.

While at university, I've also been working as an IT Advisor at Manchester Metropolitan University. I support staff and students with hardware, software, accounts and network issues, as well as working with service desk tickets, PC imaging and software deployment through SCCM.

It's given me experience dealing with problems that don't always have a straightforward answer. Sometimes that means diagnosing a hardware issue, figuring out why software isn't working, or helping someone who just needs their computer working again. It has also taught me how important it is to communicate clearly and work through problems methodically.

I've always had the same curiosity outside of college and university too. If a laptop, phone or tablet stopped working, I would usually try to figure out what was wrong before replacing it. I still approach software in much the same way. If I don't understand something, I want to take it apart and work out why.

I also became interested in financial markets and Forex trading bots. Building and experimenting with automated strategies got me interested in working with data, writing rules into software, and thinking about what happens when things don't go the way you expect. It has influenced some of the projects I've worked on and taught me to pay attention to the details rather than assuming everything will work perfectly.

I want to work with good engineers, learn from real projects, and keep getting better by building things.
